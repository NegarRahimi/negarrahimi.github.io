About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Integrative Physiology at the University of Colorado Boulder.

My research applies machine learning, deep learning, physiological signal processing, and computational modeling to the study of human movement and neuromuscular control. I work with biomedical and physiological signals including electromyography (EMG), motor-unit activity, center of pressure (CoP), force, and inertial-sensor data.

My ongoing research examines visual biofeedback during balance tasks and muscle activation during standing balance with and without visual feedback. I have also contributed to clinical research investigating the use of transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) in individuals with multiple sclerosis.

Before beginning my doctoral studies, I studied Biomedical Engineering at Amirkabir University of Technology and worked as a Research and Development Engineer on biomedical stimulation systems.
