Research profile

Research

I study human balance and movement using neurophysiology, biomedical signal processing, computational modeling, and machine learning.

I am currently pursuing my Ph.D. in the Neurophysiology of Movement Lab at the University of Colorado Boulder. My research uses machine learning, deep learning, and computational modeling to analyze biomedical and physiological signals, including electromyography (EMG) and center of pressure (CoP).

My ongoing research investigates how visual biofeedback influences balance control and muscle activation during standing tasks performed with and without visual feedback.

I have also contributed to a project investigating the effects of transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) in individuals with multiple sclerosis.

Before beginning my Ph.D., I completed my M.Sc. and B.Sc. degrees in Biomedical Engineering at Amirkabir University of Technology. My master’s research involved modeling the effects of visual deprivation during treadmill walking, while my bachelor’s research involved analyzing anti-saccade eye-movement reaction times using a custom-designed user interface.

In addition to my academic experience, I worked as a Research and Development Engineer, where I developed skills in product development and innovation, team coordination, and operational efficiency.
